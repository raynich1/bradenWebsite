Final-year project (SAIMechE award)

The short version: I developed an empirical relationship between motor current and injection pressure in the injection molding machine which I had already been working on in the lab. It was enough to earn me the annual South African Institution of Mechanical Engineering (SAIMechE) award for the best final-year project among my class.

The long version: My final-year project was based on my injection molder project which had been running for about a year prior. I decided to propose my own project instead of taking one from the list, which gave me an opportunity to devote more time to the machine.

The machine was driven by a geared AC motor for which I bought a variable frequency drive (VFD), allowing me to achieve good control. Control of the pressure applied is important in injection molding, and the pressure was related to the motor characteristics roughly by the following diagram.

Direct pressure control using a pressure transducer would have been very expensive—transducers that can handle those temperatures are highly specialized—so I devised a simpler idea. If a relationship could be derived between pressure and a controllable motor quantity, then tight control could be achieved without direct pressure measurement. In this case, measuring both motor current and linear shaft force (related to pressure by the cross-sectional area) would allow for a relationship to be derived. This would make the control diagram much simpler:

Most of the work came in setting up the testing apparatus. A custom load cell mount and custom circuitry were designed, and the variable frequency drive had to communicate with the HBM DAQ through a completely custom pin-by-pin interface. None of the infrastructure existed for this, so I had to do it myself. Electromagnetic interference from the motor was also a huge issue—I had to go to great pains to mitigate it.

The ultimate product of this looked unremarkable: a graph giving a relationship between motor current and pressure.

This was the work that won me the SAIMechE award for the best final-year thesis after I had been selected as one of six to present to the panel of professors and external examiners.
