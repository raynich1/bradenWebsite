Fracture mechanics and testing methods

The project required in-depth learning of fracture mechanics. I delved into Fracture Mechanics: Fundamentals and Applications by T.L. Anderson and quickly found out that the field is much larger than a undergraduate engineering curriculum teaches. I will not repeat the textbook here, but in short, considering material plasticity in fracture requires more intricate theories such as the J-integral. It also reconceptualizes fracture toughness as an initiation value, or where the crack begins, rather than a point at which brittle fracture occurs. Studying the field has transformed the way I think about fracture and given me a more in-depth look at related topics such as continuum mechanics. I've definitely gone further into the theory than I had to for the project, but it has been a lot of fun.

Perhaps more interesting was the development of standardized test methods. I've learned that since fracture mechanics theories get so complex, fracture mechanics testing methods has become its own field with its own challenges. The standard, while fairly comprehensive, is not flawless or complete. There is always room for improvement in measurement and analysis methods. This became a recurring theme in my dissertation, some of which became about applying novel methods to fracture toughness testing.
