Teaching

The CME runs an Honors degree program—in South Africa the degree is seen as somewhere in between a Bachelors and a Masters. In 2025 and 2026, I was responsible for teaching four weeks' worth of the class "Microstructure and Phase Transformations in Engineering Metals". Topics included an introduction to metallic structures and properties, different phases, Miller indices (directions and planes), and crystallographic texture. The class material was closely based on Materials Science and Engineering by Callister and Rethwisch.

The interesting thing about teaching this class in particular was that most of the students in the program came from scientific backgrounds like chemistry, geology, or mathematics—many of them were not familiar with a more engineering-focused program. That meant I had to explain certain concepts essentially from scratch. When going over plastic deformation mechanisms in metals, for example, I realized just how new the concept of plastic deformation was in general to many of the students. This provided a different challenge compared to teaching the mechanical engineering students, considering I could not assume a base level of engineering knowledge when I spoke to them.
